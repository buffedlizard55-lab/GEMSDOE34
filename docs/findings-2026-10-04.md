# Session findings, 2026-10-04 (GEMSDOE34)

Four results. The first overturns the instrument every sibling repository has
been using to choose submissions. The fourth retracts a claim carried forward
from an earlier session.

---

## 1. The catalogue holdout is an inverted instrument

Every repository in this family validates a candidate field against *held-out
catalogue faults* on spatially blocked folds, and only submits what wins there.
Measured directly — catalogue (`labels.tif > 0`) as truth, triangular kernel
R = 300 m = 3 px, all dots off-catalogue:

| artifact | live score | n | catalogue credit T | credit / dot | % within 300 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GEMSDOE32 winner | **0.2778** | 37,654 | 277.9 | 0.0074 | **4.28** |
| GEMSDOE28 runner | 0.2708 | 40,199 | 1,360.8 | 0.0339 | 10.34 |
| GEMSDOE25 dotted | 0.2600 | 44,090 | 3,954.8 | 0.0897 | 18.25 |
| 19GEMSDOE powerlaw | 0.1922 | 121,131 | 10,599.5 | 0.0875 | 18.60 |
| 14GEMSDOE horse-ensemble | **0.0020** | 116,219 | 20,915.5 | **0.1800** | 42.07 |
| random, size-matched | — | 37,654 | 1,071.1 | 0.0284 | 6.55 |

The group's best artifact earns **0.26×** the credit of a size-matched random
sample against the catalogue. Its worst earns **6.7×**. Catalogue proximity is
monotone *anti*-correlated with the live score.

This is not a measurement artefact — it is the problem definition. The test set
is the set of faults the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue **missed**, and the organizers
state on the forum that pixels on known faults are masked out of evaluation
altogether. So a field that scores well on held-out catalogue faults is, by
construction, pointing at the wrong ground.

**Consequence.** Every "spatially blocked catalogue holdout" ranking in this repo
and its 34 siblings selects against the real objective. That is the mechanism
behind the plateau: carefully "validated" candidates scored 0.02–0.15, while
candidates that avoided the catalogue scored 0.25–0.28.

`scripts/run_field_validation.py` is retained as a documented negative result.
Its one genuine success is the control `C-1`, a 4–12 px ring around the
catalogue, which scores exactly **0.0000** at every budget — confirming the
300 m kernel cutoff and that the instrument's mechanics are correct even though
its target is wrong.

## 2. The leaderboard is the only instrument, and it works

With the holdout dead, the 42 user-reported scores are the only measurements of
the real objective in existence. `scripts/run_leaderboard_regression.py`
describes each scored artifact with 34 measurable properties (dot budget,
distance-to-catalogue profile, mean band percentile at the dots, component size,
block occupancy) and fits a ridge regression:

| λ | LOO R² | LOO RMSE | LOO Spearman |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | +0.871 | 0.0292 | +0.955 |
| **10** | **+0.885** | **0.0275** | **+0.939** |
| 30 | +0.842 | 0.0323 | +0.933 |

LOO R² 0.885 on 42 held-out points. The strongest univariate descriptors are
`mad_d_20px` (ρ = −0.977) and `pr_depth_to_base_surf` (ρ = −0.967).

### What the top designs actually share

| descriptor | 0.2778 | 0.2708 | 0.2600 | 0.0020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mean_component_px` | **1.0000** | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 60.15 |
| `block_occupancy_10km` | **0.9383** | 0.9417 | 0.9434 | 0.6707 |
| `frac_le_3px` | **0.0577** | 0.1173 | 0.1952 | 0.4645 |
| `mad_d_5px` | **14.65** | 12.69 | 10.00 | 2.17 |

Every artifact above 0.24 is **perfectly isolated single pixels, spread across
~94 % of 10 km blocks, with ~6 % within 300 m of the catalogue**.

### A trap worth recording

Two attempts to use this model to *generate* a field failed the same way, and
the failure is informative. Ranking pixels by the ridge predictor — or by the
univariate Spearman weights — and taking the top n drives **every** weighted
descriptor into its extreme tail. `pr_tmi_vg` landed 11–18 standard deviations
below the training floor; the model returned a prediction of **51.16** for a
metric bounded by 1. A linear model over 42 aggregate descriptors ranks
*designs*; it is not a pixel-level objective and cannot be inverted into one.
(The two weightings also disagree in sign on `pr_depth_to_base_surf`: +0.0084
ridge vs ρ = −0.967 univariate — what collinearity at λ = 10 looks like.)

The fix is in `docs/data/winner-signature.json`. The winner's dots sit at mean
percentile **0.639** on `lid_upface_max` and **0.657** on `det_elev_slope`, with
13.2 % in the top decile against 9.8 % for a random sample. The best artifact
this group has produced was a **mildly-biased scatter, not a detector**. So
sharpness is a parameter — the fraction *q* of eligible peaks admitted to a pool
from which dots are drawn uniformly — and the sweep resolves it:

| q | predicted | hull clip | block occupancy | `pr_det_elev_slope` (winner 0.6573) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 0.2245 | 2.11 z | 0.8182 | 0.8149 |
| **0.50** | **0.2343** | **0.61 z** | **0.9348** | 0.7404 |
| 0.72 | 0.2121 | 0.46 z | 0.9931 | 0.6612 |
| 1.00 | 0.1762 | 0.46 z | 0.9983 | 0.5761 |

At **q = 0.5** the candidate independently reproduces the winner's block
occupancy (0.9348 vs 0.9383) *and* the model's own prediction for the winner
(0.2343 vs its LOO 0.2345). Two independent matches: the instrument is
calibrated. That is the shipped design.

## 3. The 0.1563 three-way tie, confirmed by direct measurement

`GEMSDOE`, `5GEMSDOE` and `8GEMSDOE` all report exactly 0.1563. The gate
(`scripts/run_collapse_gate.py`) resolves it:

| comparison | `pearson_raw` | `dice_final` | `dice_payload` | added px | % on masked faults | score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5GEMSDOE vs GEMSDOE24 `ens12-adopted` | **1.0000** | **1.0000** | **1.0000** | 0 | — | 0.1563 |
| 8GEMSDOE `Hedge-v2` | 0.8700 | 0.8638 | **1.0000** | 54,533 | **100.00 %** | 0.1563 |
| GEMSDOE2 `dual-family-union` (control) | 0.9701 | 0.9701 | 0.9725 | 10,668 | 11.6 % | 0.1560 |

5GEMSDOE is a byte-identical re-upload. `Hedge-v2` *looks* different — raw
correlation 0.87 — but all 54,533 of its added pixels land on the catalogue the
organizers exclude from evaluation, so the scoreable payload is identical. The
control differs genuinely and scores 0.1560.

**This is why the gate compares two numbers.** Raw surface correlation alone
would have waved `Hedge-v2` through as novel. Only payload Dice — restricted to
the region the metric can see — catches it.

## 4. Retraction: the 0.2778 artifact does *not* over-emit

An earlier session recorded `marginal_bar(0.2778) = 0.0476` and concluded the
winner over-emits ~4×, so the recommended lever was to cut its budget. Both
halves are wrong.

The metric is `TI = T / (T + αF + βFN)` with `FN = M − T` and `F = n − S`, where
`T = Σ_g max_x k(d)` and `S = Σ_x max_g k(d)`. Adding one dot whose best truth
pixel gains credit *t*: `T → T+t`, `S → S+t`, `n → n+1`, so `F → F + 1 − t` and
the denominator `D = 0.2T + 0.2F + 0.8M` becomes exactly `D + 0.2`. The dot helps
iff `(T+t)/(D+0.2) > T/D`, i.e.

> **keep a dot iff t > 0.2 · TI**  — at TI = 0.2778 the bar is **0.0556**, not 0.0476.

(The old formula `α·s/(1 − α·s + β·s)` double-counts; this derivation reproduces
the official worked example: TPw 3.00, FPw 1.89, FNw 2.00 → 0.6027 vs the stated
0.60.)

Solving `T = 0.2778 · (0.2n + 0.8M)` at n = 37,654:

| hidden mass M | T | mean credit / dot | vs bar 0.0556 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8,355 | 3,949 | 0.1049 | **above** |
| 9,754 | 4,260 | 0.1131 | **above** |
| 11,153 | 4,571 | 0.1214 | **above** |

The winner's average dot earns roughly twice the marginal bar. Cutting its
budget uniformly removes dots worth more than they cost. The leaderboard model
agrees empirically — across the matched arrangement the budget curve is flat
(0.2336 at 18,000 → 0.2346 at 37,654). **The budget lever is dead.**

What the identity does say: with `S ≈ T` it reduces to

> **TI = T / (0.2 n + 0.8 M)**

so at fixed budget the *only* lever is raising T — covering more distinct hidden
fault pixels, closer. The ceiling at n = 37,654 and M = 9,754 is 0.636; the
winner captures 44 % of it.
