Session findings, 2026-10-04 (GEMSDOE34)

Four results. The first overturns the instrument every sibling repository has been using to choose submissions. The fourth retracts a claim carried forward from an earlier session.

1. The catalogue holdout is an inverted instrument

Every repository in this family validates a candidate field against held-out catalogue faults on spatially blocked folds, and only submits what wins there. Measured directly — catalogue (labels.tif > 0) as truth, triangular kernel R = 300 m = 3 px, all dots off-catalogue:

GEMSDOE28 runner 0.2708 40,199 1,360.8 0.0339 10.34
GEMSDOE25 dotted 0.2600 44,090 3,954.8 0.0897 18.25
19GEMSDOE powerlaw 0.1922 121,131 10,599.5 0.0875 18.60
14GEMSDOE horse-ensemble 0.0020 116,219 20,915.5 0.1800 42.07
random, size-matched — 37,654 1,071.1 0.0284 6.55

The group's best artifact earns 0.26× the credit of a size-matched random sample against the catalogue. Its worst earns 6.7×. Catalogue proximity is monotone anti-correlated with the live score.

This is not a measurement artefact — it is the problem definition. The test set is the set of faults the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue missed, and the organizers state on the forum that pixels on known faults are masked out of evaluation altogether. So a field that scores well on held-out catalogue faults is, by construction, pointing at the wrong ground.

Consequence. Every "spatially blocked catalogue holdout" ranking in this repo and its 34 siblings selects against the real objective. That is the mechanism behind the plateau: carefully "validated" candidates scored 0.02–0.15, while candidates that avoided the catalogue scored 0.25–0.28.

scripts/run_field_validation.py is retained as a documented negative result. Its one genuine success is the control C-1, a 4–12 px ring around the catalogue, which scores exactly 0.0000 at every budget — confirming the 300 m kernel cutoff and that the instrument's mechanics are correct even though its target is wrong.

A trap worth recording

Two attempts to use this model to generate a field failed the same way, and the failure is informative. Ranking pixels by the ridge predictor — or by the univariate Spearman weights — and taking the top n drives every weighted descriptor into its extreme tail. pr_tmi_vg landed 11–18 standard deviations below the training floor; the model returned a prediction of 51.16 for a metric bounded by 1. A linear model over 42 aggregate descriptors ranks designs; it is not a pixel-level objective and cannot be inverted into one. (The two weightings also disagree in sign on pr_depth_to_base_surf: +0.0084 ridge vs ρ = −0.967 univariate — what collinearity at λ = 10 looks like.)

The fix is in docs/data/winner-signature.json. The winner's dots sit at mean percentile 0.639 on lid_upface_max and 0.657 on det_elev_slope, with 13.2 % in the top decile against 9.8 % for a random sample. The best artifact this group has produced was a mildly-biased scatter, not a detector. So sharpness is a parameter — the fraction q of eligible peaks admitted to a pool from which dots are drawn uniformly — and the sweep resolves it:

4. Retraction: the 0.2778 artifact does not over-emit

An earlier session recorded marginal_bar(0.2778) = 0.0476 and concluded the winner over-emits ~4×, so the recommended lever was to cut its budget. Both halves are wrong.

The metric is TI = T / (T + αF + βFN) with FN = M − T and F = n − S, where T = Σ_g max_x k(d) and S = Σ_x max_g k(d). Adding one dot whose best truth pixel gains credit t: T → T+t, S → S+t, n → n+1, so F → F + 1 − t and the denominator D = 0.2T + 0.2F + 0.8M becomes exactly D + 0.2. The dot helps iff (T+t)/(D+0.2) > T/D, i.e.

keep a dot iff t > 0.2 · TI — at TI = 0.2778 the bar is 0.0556, not 0.0476.

(The old formula α·s/(1 − α·s + β·s) double-counts; this derivation reproduces the official worked example: TPw 3.00, FPw 1.89, FNw 2.00 → 0.6027 vs the stated 0.60.)

Solving T = 0.2778 · (0.2n + 0.8M) at n = 37,654:

hidden mass M T mean credit / dot vs bar 0.0556
8,355 3,949 0.1049 above
9,754 4,260 0.1131 above
11,153 4,571 0.1214 above

The winner's average dot earns roughly twice the marginal bar. Cutting its budget uniformly removes dots worth more than they cost. The leaderboard model agrees empirically — across the matched arrangement the budget curve is flat (0.2336 at 18,000 → 0.2346 at 37,654). The budget lever is dead.

What the identity does say: with S ≈ T it reduces to

TI = T / (0.2 n + 0.8 M)

so at fixed budget the only lever is raising T — covering more distinct hidden fault pixels, closer. The ceiling at n = 37,654 and M = 9,754 is 0.636; the winner captures 44 % of it.
